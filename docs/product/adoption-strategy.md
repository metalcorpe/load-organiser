# Skydiver-first adoption strategy

Status: working direction proposed by the product owner; discovery continues. No MVP, launch plan, or participating dropzones are assumed.

The [connected product model](product-model.md) maps all feature IDs to shared records, user journeys, and participation dependencies.

## Initial audience and product promise

Target individual skydivers first, then seek dropzone adoption. The proposed initial promise is: keep your jumping history, equipment records, and flight data useful wherever you jump, including places that do not use the application.

This changes the earlier operations-first sequencing. A complete manifest product and two cooperating dropzones are not prerequisites for personal use. Existing operational code remains relevant to the longer-term ecosystem, but its defects should not determine which personal needs to investigate first.

## Participation dependencies

| Can provide value independently | Needs another person's participation | Needs operator participation or integration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual logbook, statistics, history imports, and export | New instructor/coach confirmations | Live manifest and guaranteed load assignments |
| Personal equipment records and reminders | Attributable rigger service confirmations | Accepted local check-in and eligibility decisions |
| FlySight/track uploads and supported debrief charts | Shared private team plans and coaching feedback | Confirmed bookings against real availability |
| Forecasts from a licensed external provider | A current coach/rigger availability directory | Automated completed-jump records from operations |
| Personal goals, notes, document storage, and visit preparation | Accepted course/event registration | Dropzone-issued status updates and operational announcements |
| Source-linked events/boogies/courses board and school/provider discovery | Organizer-maintained listings and answered coaching inquiries | Confirmed registration, payment, and inventory synchronized with a provider |

Independent does not mean dependency-free: forecasts need a provider, imports need supported files, and cloud storage has operating costs. Personal records can store existing evidence without requiring the original issuer to join; new digital verification needs an attributable verifier.

Support visiting a nonparticipating location without representing it as a partner. A destination listing, saved visit, external booking link, or personal plan is not a confirmed booking. Personal recent-activity summaries do not establish acceptance by a dropzone.

## Candidate reasons to return

- After a jump: record it, import telemetry, attach media, and add notes.
- Between jumping days: review progress, equipment usage, and service reminders.
- Before a visit: review forecasts, prepare records, and export/share selected information.
- When planning activities: browse the [discovery board](discovery-board.md), save relevant courses/events, and receive opted-in changes.
- With friends: follow permitted shared activity and optionally share visit intentions. Private logging and plans remain private unless published; see [social activity](social-activity.md).

These are retention hypotheses, not measured behavior. Discovery should establish whether one solves a frequent enough problem to motivate adoption rather than selecting all of them for an initial release.

## Initial segments to investigate

- Regular licensed jumpers seeking convenient logging and equipment history.
- Traveling jumpers seeking portable records and visit preparation.
- Device users seeking import and debrief workflows.
- Students/progressing jumpers seeking goals and organized feedback.

The segment is not selected. Device analysis, training progression, and travel preparation may require different product emphasis; record these differences before narrowing scope.

## Later path to dropzone adoption

1. Individuals maintain useful portable records independently.
2. They can share a readable summary or export without obliging the recipient to adopt the application.
3. Interested coaches, riggers, or staff may participate in narrowly scoped record confirmation or review workflows.
4. A dropzone may adopt record intake, check-in, event registration, or booking before considering the full manifest system.
5. Operators may connect local operations to the shared network where there is demonstrated value.

Each transition is a hypothesis. Individual usage does not guarantee operator adoption. Staff workload, accepted evidence, local control, integration effort, and business value need separate investigation. Any operator workspace must enforce dropzone isolation and local staff roles before use.

## Business and discovery questions

See [sustainability and community support](sustainability.md) for the proposed free-core/support model, alternatives to advertising, and the need to measure cash costs and maintenance effort separately. No funding plan or prices are selected.

- Which personal workflow would users choose over their current tool, and why?
- Can historical import and record export reduce the effort and perceived cost of switching?
- Which features are valuable without signatures, local acceptance, or live inventory?
- What are the ongoing costs of weather, storage, uploads, and support?
- Would a personal subscription, optional advanced tools, or another model sustain that value? No model is selected.
- What specific additional benefit could later motivate a dropzone to participate?

Continue documenting these needs and alternatives. Do not infer willingness to pay or network growth from the presence of similar products. Future research could examine repeated personal use and usefulness of shared summaries before attempting a partner-dependent travel pilot.
